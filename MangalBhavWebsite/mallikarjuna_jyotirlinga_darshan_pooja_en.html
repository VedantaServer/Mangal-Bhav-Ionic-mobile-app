Understanding the Jyotirlinga

🕉️ What is a Jyotirlinga?

According to the Shiva Purana, the twelve sacred sites where Lord Shiva himself manifested in the form of Jyoti (radiant light) are called the Dwadash Jyotirlinga. Spread across India, they are regarded as the supreme pilgrimage sites for devotees of Shiva.

Among these, Shri Mallikarjuna holds the honor of being the second Jyotirlinga — seated atop the Srisailam mountain in Andhra Pradesh, amid the Nallamala forests, on the banks of the holy Krishna river (Patala Ganga). It is the only pilgrimage site in India where both a Jyotirlinga and a Shakti Peeth (Maa Bhramaramba) are enshrined together. It is believed that darshan and abhishek here fulfill all wishes and bring the combined grace of both Shiva and Shakti.

Journeys to the remaining eleven Jyotirlingas are also available on Mangal Bhav one by one. Tap a number on the wheel to learn about each Jyotirlinga.

Mythological Significance

🌿 The religious significance of Mallikarjuna

Layered with countless strands of history and faith, this temple holds a special place for devotees of Shiva—

🕉️
Kartikeya's quest and the arrival of Shiva-Parvati
According to legend, after losing a marriage contest to Ganesha, Kartikeya departed for Kraunch mountain. To console their son, Lord Shiva and Parvati themselves came to Srisailam and settled here forever as Mallikarjuna (Shiva) and Bhramaramba (Parvati).
🏞️
Situated on the banks of the holy Krishna river (Patala Ganga)
The temple stands on Srisailam mountain within the Nallamala forest region, on the banks of the holy Krishna river — worshipped here also as "Patala Ganga."
👑
Dravidian architecture and ancient history
The present temple's tall ramparts and gopurams are believed to have been built during the Vijayanagara empire, making it one of South India's oldest and grandest Dravidian-style temples.
🏛️
A rare confluence of Jyotirlinga and Shakti Peeth
Srisailam is the only pilgrimage site in India where one of the twelve Jyotirlingas (Mallikarjuna) and one of the eighteen Shakti Peeths (Bhramaramba) are enshrined together in the same complex.
🐅
Amid the Nallamala forest and tiger reserve
The temple stands amid the dense forests of the Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve — reached via winding ghat roads, the journey itself is a unique experience.
📜
A place in the Shiva Purana and the Dwadash Jyotirlinga Stotram
Mallikarjuna holds second place among the twelve Jyotirlingas described in the Koti Rudra Samhita of the Shiva Purana, and it was here that Adi Shankaracharya composed the Shivananda Lahari.
Why Mallikarjuna

Mallikarjuna, seated atop the Srisailam mountain in Andhra Pradesh, amid the Nallamala forests on the banks of the Krishna river, is a sacred site the scriptures place second among the twelve Jyotirlingas and hold as exceptionally special — for it is the only site where a Jyotirlinga and a Shakti Peeth converge. Amid the dense forests and the sacred flow of the Krishna, the tradition of Shiva-Shakti worship here has continued unbroken across the ages.

Devotees from India and abroad come here to receive the joint blessings of Lord Mallikarjuna and Maa Bhramaramba, and perform Rudrabhishek and darshan pooja according to scriptural rites.
