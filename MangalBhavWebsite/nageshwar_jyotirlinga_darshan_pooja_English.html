Understanding the Jyotirlinga

🕉️ What is a Jyotirlinga?

According to the Shiva Purana, the twelve sacred sites where Lord Shiva manifested himself as a form of divine light are called the twelve Jyotirlingas. Spread across India, they are regarded as the most sacred pilgrimage sites for devotees of Shiva.

Among these, Shri Nageshwar is worshipped as the tenth Jyotirlinga, situated on the route between Dwarka and Beyt Dwarka in Gujarat. "Nageshwar" means the Lord of the Nagas (serpents), a deity believed to protect devotees from poison, fear, and enemies. Darshan and Abhishek here are believed to remove fear and hardship and bring blessings of protection.

The pilgrimage to the remaining eleven Jyotirlingas will also become available on Mangal Bhav one by one. Tap a number on the wheel to learn about each Jyotirlinga.

Puranic Significance

🌿 Religious Significance of Nageshwar

Layered with countless strands of history and faith, this temple holds a special place for devotees of Shiva—

🐍
Slaying of the Demon Daaruka
Legend has it that a demon named Daaruka held the devotee Supriya captive; Lord Shiva manifested as Nageshwar, slayed the demon and protected the devotee — hence the name "Nageshwar," the Lord of the Nagas.
🛡️
Protector from Poison and Fear
Devotees believe that darshan and worship of Nageshwar Mahadev protect against fear of snakes, poison, and enemies.
🗿
Massive Shiva Statue
The magnificent 25-metre-tall statue of Lord Shiva within the temple complex is visible to devotees from afar and gives Nageshwar its distinct identity.
📜
Mentioned in the Shiva Purana
The Shiva Purana and Skanda Purana describe Nageshwar as the tenth among the twelve Jyotirlingas.
🧭
A Sacred Site Near Dwarka
Located a short distance from the Dwarkadhish Temple on the Beyt Dwarka route, devotees often combine their Dwarka pilgrimage with Nageshwar darshan.
🙏
Faith and Protection
Devotees believe that darshan and abhishek here bring freedom from fear, inner peace and a sense of protection in life.
